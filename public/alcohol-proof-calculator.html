Distilling and mixology both come down to one number: how strong is this liquid, really? An Alcohol Proof Calculator converts that number in both directions — proof to ABV, ABV to proof, and strength to strength — so you can dilute a high-proof distillate, correct a hydrometer reading for temperature, or work out how many standard drinks are sitting in a bottle without guessing. Whether you're a home distiller cutting a fresh run down to bottle strength or you just want to know how many drinks are in a 25-ounce bottle of wine, the formulas below turn proof, alcohol content, and volume into numbers you can actually use.

Alcohol Proof Calculator Basics: Proofing Calculator Math Explained

In the United States, proof is simply double the alcohol by volume: 80-proof whiskey is 40% ABV, and 100-proof rum is 50% ABV. Converting proof to ABV (or back again) is the first calculation behind almost every other tool on this page, from a dilution calculator to a full distilling calculator suite. Proof exists because early distillers "proved" a spirit's strength by soaking gunpowder in it — if the powder still ignited, the spirit was at least 100 proof under the original British standard, roughly 57% ABV. The American system simplified that to a flat 2:1 ratio, which is why the same proof converter works whether you're checking whiskey, rum, vodka, or any other distilled spirits.

$$\text{ABV} = \frac{\text{Proof}}{2} \qquad \text{Proof} = \text{ABV} \times 2$$

Why Distillers, Bartenders, and Regulators Track Proof

A bar needs proof to price a pour, a distillery needs it to pay the right federal excise tax, and a home distiller needs it to know a batch is safe and legal to drink. Tracking alcohol content this precisely is also what keeps a fortification recipe, a mingling spirits blend, or a sugar wash calculation reliable from one run to the next.

Convert Proof to Alc/Vol

Most alcoholmeters are calibrated for 20°C — a warm sample reads falsely high and a cold one reads falsely low, so you correct the reading before you trust it. That correction step is exactly what "convert proof to alc/vol" means in practice: adjust for temperature first, then apply the 2:1 ratio above to move between proof and Alc/Vol.

Alcohol Dilution Proofing Calculator: Cutting Spirits to Bottle Strength

Once you know a spirit's proof, the next question is almost always the same: how much water do you add to bring a high-proof distillate down to bottle strength? An alcohol dilution proofing calculator answers that with a simple ratio, and the same math scales from a 50 mL test sample up to a batch measured in liters. Whether you're measuring in fluid ounces, cups, pints, quarts, gallons, or milliliters, only the units change — the ratio stays the same.

Dilution Calculator Formula (Pearson's Square)

The dilution calculator formula is a simplified Pearson's square: multiply your starting volume by your starting strength, divide by your target strength, and the result is your final volume. Subtract the starting volume from that final volume and you have exactly how much water to add.

$$V_{\text{final}} = V_{\text{start}} \times \frac{A_{\text{start}}}{A_{\text{target}}}$$

For example, diluting 1 liter of 95% alcohol down to 40% needs a final volume of 2.375 liters — meaning you'd add 1.375 liters of water. These dilution ratios scale linearly, so doubling every input just doubles the water needed too.

Starting volume1 liter
Current alcohol95% ABV
Desired alcohol40% ABV
Spirit alcohol added (if fortifying)0% (water only)
Final volume after dilution2.375 liters
Water to add1.375 liters

Distillate to Water Calculator: Reaching Bottle Strength From a Fresh Run

A distillate to water calculator is really the same dilution formula pointed at a specific job: taking a fresh, high-proof run off your still and cutting it down to a drinkable, sellable bottle strength — typically 40-50% ABV for spirits and lower for a flavored liqueur. Run it through your column, note the proof coming off the condenser, then use the distillate to water calculator above to figure out exactly how many fluid ounces or milliliters of water to add before you bottle.

Sugar Wash Calculator and Distilling Calculator Basics for Home Distillers

Whether you call it a hobby, a craft, or moonshine made the old-fashioned way, every batch starts as a sugar wash — sugar, water, and yeast fermenting into low-proof alcohol. A sugar wash calculator estimates that starting alcohol content from your hydrometer readings, which is the number every distilling calculator downstream depends on.

Sugar Wash Calculator: Estimating the Alcohol Content of the Wash

Take an SG (specific gravity) reading before fermentation and another after, plug both into the sugar wash calculator, and the difference in the alcohol content of the wash tells you your starting strength before distillation — typically 8-12% for a basic wash, higher with a turbo yeast built for higher-alcohol worts.

StageAlcohol Content (ABV)
Sugar wash before distillation8-12%
Low wines (first distillate run)20-30%
Final distillate (spirit run)60-80%

Boil Time, Vapor Speed, and Temperature Converter Tools

The rest of a distilling calculator suite exists to keep your still running efficiently. A boil time calculator uses your wash volume, heating power (in watts or BTU/h), and starting temperature to estimate time to temp before vapor even starts rising. A vapor speed calculator factors in your column diameter and power input to estimate how fast vapor moves through a column still — too fast and you lose separation between heads, hearts, and tails. Because most alcoholmeters are calibrated at 20°C, a temperature converter that moves between degrees Celsius and degrees Fahrenheit keeps every corrected ABV reading accurate no matter what thermometer you're holding. A well-insulated still improves efficiency and shortens the time to temp for your next run — most distilling calculators bundle all three of these into one page, but breaking them out here makes each formula easier to follow.

Mingling Spirits Calculator and Fortification (Spirit Addition)

Sometimes the question isn't dilution but blending: combining two batches at different strengths, or adding a stronger spirit to a wine to fortify it. A mingling spirits calculator and a fortification calculator both solve the same underlying problem — hitting one target alcohol percentage by combining two known ones — while keeping every batch within compliance and consistency, batch after batch.

Mingling Spirits Calculator: Blending Batches to a Target Proof

Say you have 10 liters at 60% ABV and 5 liters at 30% ABV. This tool weights each batch by volume and strength to tell you the combined ABV before you ever pour them together — useful for anyone managing bottles across a production run, not just a single dilution.

Fortification: Boosting Wine ABV with a Dilution Water Calculator

Fortifying a wine means adding a higher-proof spirit to raise its final ABV — the reverse of dilution, but the same three numbers: current alcohol, spirit alcohol, and desired alcohol. The same dilution water calculator math applies whether you're fortifying a wine or adjusting a finished spirit down instead of up.

Alcohol Drink Size Calculator: Standard Drinks in Beer, Wine, and Malt Liquor

Standard drinks — also called alcoholic drink equivalents — are what let you compare a beer, a glass of wine, and a shot of distilled spirits on equal terms. Run the numbers through the alcohol drink size calculator whenever a label doesn't state standard drinks directly.

Half-Pint of 80-Proof Distilled Spirits? Reading a Real Container

A 21-ounce ballpark souvenir cup of beer, a 40-ounce bottle of malt liquor, a half-pint of 80-proof distilled spirits, and a 25-ounce bottle of wine can each hold a very different number of standard drinks, even though every one of them looks like "one drink" on the label. That's the gap this calculator closes: it turns container size and alcohol content into a number you can actually compare.

Standard Drinks in a Cocktail Recipe: Using the Calculator for Compliance

The same math works in reverse for a cocktail recipe: if you know the proof of each spirit going into the glass and the pour size, you can back into the total standard drinks per serving — useful for a bar tracking compliance as much as for a home bartender keeping an eye on their own drinking.

  • Alcohol content and ABV describe the same percentage of pure ethanol in a drink — proof is just that number doubled.
  • Beer, wine, malt liquor, and distilled spirits carry very different alcohol by volume percentages, so container size alone never tells you the number of standard drinks.
  • This calculator estimates alcohol content only; it cannot calculate your blood alcohol concentration and should never be used to decide whether it's safe to drive after drinking.
  • Always confirm your local legal drinking age and container size rules before scaling a fortification or dilution recipe for personal use.

Whether that spirit came off traditional copper stills or a modern reflux column, whiskey, rum, and moonshine all reduce to the same two numbers — proof and volume. That's the whole job of an alcohol proof calculator: turn one strength number into whatever unit or format you actually need.
